Holes Chapters 1-8

Larger student-readable summaries with discussion questions for the assigned reading.

Go Deeper: Chapters 9-18

Chapter 1

Chapter 1 introduces the setting of the book: Camp Green Lake. At first, the name sounds like a nice place. A reader might imagine trees, water, grass, and a peaceful camp. But the narrator quickly tells us that Camp Green Lake is not green, and there is no lake. The lake dried up more than a hundred years ago.

Now Camp Green Lake is a dry, empty, dangerous place. During the day, the sun beats down, and there is almost no shade. The land is flat and hot. The narrator describes dangerous animals that live there, including rattlesnakes and scorpions. But the most dangerous creatures are yellow-spotted lizards. The narrator warns that if one bites you, you will die.

This chapter is short, but it is important because it creates the mood of the story. The setting feels harsh, lonely, and unsafe. The author wants the reader to understand right away that Camp Green Lake is not a normal camp. It is a place where survival will be difficult.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why is the name "Camp Green Lake" misleading?
  2. What details make the setting feel dangerous?
  3. What mood does the author create in this chapter?
  4. Why do you think the author begins with the setting instead of Stanley?

Chapter 2

Chapter 2 explains what Camp Green Lake really is. It is not a fun summer camp. It is a camp for boys who have gotten in trouble with the law. Boys are sent there instead of going to jail.

The narrator explains that there are no fences around Camp Green Lake. At first, this may seem surprising. A detention camp sounds like it should have fences or guards everywhere. But the narrator explains that fences are not needed. Camp Green Lake is surrounded by desert. If a boy tries to run away, he will have no water, no shade, and nowhere safe to go. The land itself keeps the boys trapped.

The boys at Camp Green Lake have to dig one hole every day. Each hole must be five feet deep and five feet wide. The shovel is used to measure the hole. The adults say that digging holes builds character. However, the way the narrator explains it makes the reader suspicious. Digging holes sounds exhausting and painful, and it may not really be about building character at all.

This chapter introduces one of the main mysteries of the book: why are the boys digging holes every day?

Discussion Questions

  1. Why does Camp Green Lake not need fences?
  2. What do the boys have to do every day?
  3. What do the adults claim digging holes does?
  4. Do you believe digging holes is only meant to build character? Why or why not?

Chapter 3

Chapter 3 introduces Stanley Yelnats, the main character. Stanley's last name is special because it is a palindrome. That means it is spelled the same forward and backward: Yelnats is Stanley spelled backward.

Stanley is not a popular or confident boy. He is overweight, and other kids often tease him. He does not have many friends. He is also used to bad luck. In fact, Stanley's family believes they are cursed. Whenever something bad happens, they blame Stanley's "no-good-dirty-rotten-pig-stealing-great-great-grandfather."

Stanley's family is poor, but they are hopeful. His father is an inventor who is trying to find a way to recycle old sneakers. His inventions usually fail, and the apartment smells terrible because of the old shoes. Still, Stanley's family keeps trying.

Stanley is sent to Camp Green Lake because he is accused of stealing a pair of shoes that belonged to a famous baseball player named Clyde "Sweet Feet" Livingston. Stanley says he did not steal them. He claims the shoes fell from the sky. This sounds impossible, so no one believes him.

This chapter helps us understand Stanley before he gets to camp. He seems unlucky, lonely, and misunderstood. The reader may begin to feel that Stanley is being punished for something he did not do.

Discussion Questions

  1. What do we learn about Stanley's personality and life?
  2. Why is Stanley's last name interesting?
  3. What does Stanley's family blame their bad luck on?
  4. Why does Stanley's story about the shoes sound unbelievable?
  5. Do you think Stanley is guilty or innocent? What makes you think so?

Chapter 4

Chapter 4 shows Stanley arriving at Camp Green Lake. He meets Mr. Sir, one of the adults in charge. Mr. Sir is strict and unpleasant. He tells Stanley that Camp Green Lake is not a Girl Scout camp. This means Stanley should not expect kindness, comfort, or fun.

Mr. Sir explains that Stanley will dig a hole every day, including Saturdays and Sundays. Each hole must be five feet deep and five feet wide. If Stanley finds anything interesting or unusual while digging, he is supposed to report it to the adults.

Mr. Sir also tells Stanley that running away would be foolish. There is no water for miles. This makes Stanley understand that the camp does not need fences because the desert is already a prison.

Stanley is given two sets of orange clothes, a towel, and a canteen. He is assigned to Group D. Everything about the camp feels harsh, controlled, and uncomfortable. Stanley is beginning to realize that Camp Green Lake will be much worse than he imagined.

Discussion Questions

  1. What impression does Mr. Sir make when Stanley arrives?
  2. What rules does Stanley learn?
  3. Why would running away be dangerous?
  4. Why does Mr. Sir tell Stanley to report anything interesting he finds?
  5. What does this chapter reveal about the camp's power over the boys?

Chapter 5

Chapter 5 introduces the boys in Stanley's group. Stanley meets Mr. Pendanski, who seems nicer than Mr. Sir, though he still works for the camp. Mr. Pendanski tells Stanley that the boys in Group D are not bad boys. He says they have made bad choices.

Stanley meets several boys with unusual nicknames: X-Ray, Armpit, Squid, Zigzag, Magnet, and Zero. These names sound strange, but at camp they are normal. The boys do not use their regular names very much. Nicknames are part of the camp culture.

Stanley is given the nickname "Caveman." He does not really choose this name, but he accepts it because he wants to fit in. Stanley is still new and unsure of himself. He does not know who has power in the group or what rules the boys follow among themselves.

This chapter is important because Stanley is not only learning the official rules of the camp. He is also learning the social rules. To survive at Camp Green Lake, Stanley will need to understand the other boys, their friendships, their attitudes, and their hidden ranking system.

Discussion Questions

  1. How is Mr. Pendanski different from Mr. Sir?
  2. What are some of the boys' nicknames?
  3. Why might the boys use nicknames instead of real names?
  4. Why does Stanley accept the nickname "Caveman"?
  5. What does Stanley need to learn about the other boys?

Chapter 6

Chapter 6 explains more about why Stanley was sent to Camp Green Lake. Stanley was accused of stealing shoes that belonged to Clyde "Sweet Feet" Livingston, a famous baseball player. The shoes were valuable because they were going to be auctioned for charity.

Stanley says he did not steal the shoes. He says he was walking home one day when the shoes fell from the sky and hit him. He took them because he thought it was a sign. His father was working on a sneaker invention, so Stanley thought the shoes might help.

Unfortunately, the police saw Stanley with the shoes and arrested him. Stanley's explanation sounded ridiculous. Shoes do not usually fall from the sky. Even though Stanley was telling the truth, the court did not believe him. He was given a choice: go to jail or go to Camp Green Lake. Stanley had never been to camp before, so he chose Camp Green Lake.

This chapter shows how unfair Stanley's situation is. He is punished because the evidence makes him look guilty, even though he did not actually steal the shoes. It also continues the theme of bad luck. Stanley's life seems shaped by strange accidents and misunderstandings.

Discussion Questions

  1. What was Stanley accused of stealing?
  2. Why were the shoes important?
  3. What really happened to the shoes?
  4. Why did Stanley's explanation sound unbelievable?
  5. Was Stanley treated fairly? Why or why not?

Chapter 7

Chapter 7 is one of the longest and most important early chapters. Stanley digs his first hole at Camp Green Lake. At first, he thinks the shovel does not look too big, but he quickly learns that digging is extremely difficult. The ground is hard, the sun is hot, and Stanley's body is not used to this kind of work.

As he digs, Stanley gets blisters on his hands. He becomes exhausted and thirsty. The hole must be five feet deep and five feet wide, which is much harder than he expected. The other boys are more experienced, and Stanley realizes how weak and unprepared he is compared with them.

This chapter also begins the story of Stanley's great-great-grandfather, Elya Yelnats. Elya lived in Latvia and was in love with a girl named Myra. He wanted to marry her, but another man also wanted to marry her. Elya went to Madame Zeroni for advice. Madame Zeroni gave him a small pig and told him to carry it up a mountain every day to drink from a stream. As the pig grew stronger, Elya would grow stronger too.

Madame Zeroni warned Elya that after she helped him, he must carry her up the mountain and sing to her. If he failed, his family would be cursed. Elya followed part of the plan, but later he forgot his promise to Madame Zeroni. This is where the family curse is believed to have started.

Chapter 7 connects Stanley's suffering in the present with his family history. Stanley is digging holes in the desert, while the story from the past explains why his family believes they have terrible luck.

Discussion Questions

  1. Why is Stanley's first hole so difficult?
  2. What physical problems does Stanley experience while digging?
  3. How does Stanley compare himself to the other boys?
  4. Who is Elya Yelnats?
  5. What does Madame Zeroni tell Elya to do?
  6. Why is Elya's broken promise important?
  7. How does the past story connect to Stanley's present situation?

Chapter 8

Chapter 8 returns to the dangers of Camp Green Lake. The narrator gives more information about yellow-spotted lizards. These lizards are small but deadly. They have yellow spots, black teeth, and white tongues. They live in holes, which is especially important because the boys spend all day digging holes.

The narrator explains that yellow-spotted lizards are very dangerous. If one bites you, you will die a slow and painful death. The description is almost funny because it is so direct, but it is also scary. The author uses this chapter to remind readers that Camp Green Lake is not just uncomfortable. It is life-threatening.

This chapter builds suspense. Readers now know that the boys are digging in a place where deadly creatures may live. The chapter also makes the title of the book feel more important. Holes are not just punishment. Holes may hide danger, secrets, and mysteries.

Discussion Questions

  1. What do yellow-spotted lizards look like?
  2. Why are they dangerous?
  3. Why is it important that the lizards live in holes?
  4. How does this chapter make Camp Green Lake feel more threatening?
  5. Why might the author include this warning so early in the book?

Whole Assignment Discussion Questions

  1. How does Louis Sachar make Camp Green Lake feel like a prison even without fences?
    Answer: He shows that the desert traps the boys. There is no water, shade, or safe place to run.
  2. Is Stanley unlucky, treated unfairly, or both?
    Answer: Both. Strange things happen to him, but people also judge him without understanding the truth.
  3. Why might the adults really want the boys to dig holes?
    Answer: The official reason is to build character, but the repeated digging and the rule about reporting interesting objects suggest they may be looking for something.
  4. What kind of person is Stanley at the beginning of the book?
    Answer: He is lonely, insecure, unlucky, and used to being blamed, but he is also thoughtful and not cruel.
  5. What are the biggest mysteries so far?
    Answer: Why the boys are really digging holes, whether the curse is real, what happened with the shoes, and what secrets Camp Green Lake may be hiding.
