Literature Analysis Summary Packet

Holes, Chapters 19-29 — teacher summaries, student-reader summaries, and board words.

Volunteer reader routine: Assign one student to each chapter. The student writes the chapter's board word, reads the summary aloud, and then the class answers one question. The board words can be reused in the word search.

Holes — Chapters 19-29

Chapter 19

Teacher — 1 sentence

Stanley is blamed for the sunflower seeds after Magnet steals them.

Student reader — board word: SEEDS
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

Mr. Sir brings a sack of sunflower seeds to the lake. Magnet steals the sack and tosses it around, but the seeds spill into Stanley's hole. Stanley accepts blame because he does not want to turn against the other boys.

QuestionListen For
Why does Stanley take the blame for the seeds?He wants to protect Magnet and avoid turning against the group, even though it makes Mr. Sir angry at him.
What does this event show about the pressure among the boys?The boys are under pressure to stay loyal to each other, even when someone else caused the problem.
How does Stanley's choice affect Mr. Sir's attitude toward him?Mr. Sir becomes more hostile toward Stanley and takes him to the Warden.

Chapter 20

Teacher — 1 sentence

The Warden punishes Mr. Sir instead of Stanley, showing her frightening control.

Student reader — board word: VENOM
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

Mr. Sir takes Stanley to the Warden, expecting Stanley to be punished. Instead, the Warden hurts Mr. Sir with her rattlesnake-venom nail polish. Stanley sees that the Warden is dangerous and unpredictable.

QuestionListen For
Why does Mr. Sir take Stanley to the Warden?He wants Stanley punished for the sunflower seeds and thinks the Warden will side with him.
How does the Warden surprise both Mr. Sir and Stanley?She hurts Mr. Sir with her venom nail polish instead of punishing Stanley.
What does this chapter show about power at Camp Green Lake?The Warden has frightening power over everyone, including the counselors.

Chapter 21

Teacher — 1 sentence

Stanley thinks about God's Thumb and survival in the desert.

Student reader — board word: THUMB
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

Stanley returns to his hole shaken by what happened. He remembers the story of his great-grandfather surviving in the desert after finding refuge on God's Thumb. This memory matters because Stanley is beginning to connect family stories, survival, and the land around Camp Green Lake.

QuestionListen For
What old family story does Stanley remember?He remembers that his great-grandfather survived in the desert after finding refuge on God's Thumb.
Why might God's Thumb matter in this part of the book?It may connect Stanley's family history to the land around Camp Green Lake.
How does the setting connect the past and present?The desert, mountain, and old family story begin to feel linked to Stanley's current situation.

Chapter 22

Teacher — 1 sentence

Stanley and Zero make a deal: reading lessons for digging help.

Student reader — board word: TEACH
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

Zero asks Stanley to teach him to read. Stanley agrees, and Zero offers to dig part of Stanley's hole in exchange. Their private deal begins a real friendship, even though the other boys do not yet understand it.

QuestionListen For
Why does Zero want Stanley to teach him?Zero cannot read and wants to learn, even though others underestimate him.
Why does Stanley agree?Zero offers to dig part of Stanley's hole, and Stanley begins to see Zero differently.
How does this deal change their relationship?They move from barely speaking to helping each other, beginning a real friendship.

Chapter 23

Teacher — 1 sentence

The book begins the Green Lake past timeline with Katherine and Sam.

Student reader — board word: SCHOOL
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

The story shifts back in time to the old town of Green Lake. Miss Katherine Barlow teaches in the schoolhouse, and Sam sells onions from his boat. This timeline shows that Green Lake used to be full of water, people, and ordinary daily life.

QuestionListen For
How is old Green Lake different from Camp Green Lake?Old Green Lake had water, a town, a school, and ordinary life; Camp Green Lake is dry and punishing.
Who are Katherine Barlow and Sam?Katherine is the schoolteacher, and Sam sells onions and helps repair things.
Why does the author move into the past here?The past timeline explains the place, the mystery, and the buried history readers need.

Chapter 24

Teacher — 1 sentence

Mr. Sir punishes Stanley by withholding water.

Student reader — board word: WATER
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

Back in the present, Mr. Sir is still angry about what the Warden did to him. He takes out his anger on Stanley by refusing to give him water. Stanley chooses not to complain, showing how powerless he feels.

QuestionListen For
Why is Mr. Sir angry at Stanley?He blames Stanley for what happened with the Warden and the sunflower seeds.
Why is water such a serious punishment in this setting?The camp is dangerously hot and dry, so withholding water is cruel and dangerous.
What does Stanley's silence show?It shows he feels powerless and knows complaining may make things worse.

Chapter 25

Teacher — 1 sentence

Katherine and Sam grow closer through the schoolhouse repairs and onion visits.

Student reader — board word: ONIONS
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

In the past, Katherine and Sam spend more time together as Sam repairs the schoolhouse and sells onions. Their friendship grows into love, but their town does not accept a relationship between a white woman and a Black man. The past timeline begins to show danger beneath the peaceful lake town.

QuestionListen For
How does Sam help Katherine?He sells onions and repairs the schoolhouse, giving them time to talk and know each other.
What feelings begin to grow between Katherine and Sam?They begin to love each other.
Why is their relationship dangerous in their town?The racist town rejects a relationship between a white woman and a Black man.

Chapter 26

Teacher — 1 sentence

Katherine Barlow becomes Kissin' Kate Barlow after Sam is killed.

Student reader — board word: OUTLAW
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

After Katherine and Sam are discovered, a racist mob destroys the schoolhouse and kills Sam. Katherine is heartbroken and furious. She later becomes the outlaw Kissin' Kate Barlow, showing how grief and injustice change her life.

QuestionListen For
What happens to Sam?A mob kills Sam after his relationship with Katherine is discovered.
How does this tragedy change Katherine?Her grief and anger help turn her into the outlaw Kissin' Kate Barlow.
How does this past event change the reader's understanding of Camp Green Lake?Readers see that the dry lake bed has a history of love, racism, violence, and buried pain.

Chapter 27

Teacher — 1 sentence

Zero's reading lessons continue, and Stanley learns Zero is strong in math.

Student reader — board word: MATH
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

In the present, Zero becomes better at reading, and Stanley discovers that Zero is very good at math. Zero keeps digging part of Stanley's hole, which makes the other boys jealous and angry. Stanley starts to see Zero as intelligent, not empty.

QuestionListen For
What does Stanley learn about Zero?Zero is not stupid; he is determined, observant, and very good at math.
Why do the other boys resent the deal?They think Zero is doing Stanley's work and giving Stanley an unfair advantage.
How does point of view affect how we understand Zero?Readers see more of Zero's abilities as Stanley gets to know him, while other boys still judge him.

Chapter 28

Teacher — 1 sentence

Kissin' Kate Barlow connects Stanley's family history to the buried treasure mystery.

Student reader — board word: KATE
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

The past timeline returns to Kissin' Kate Barlow. She robbed Stanley's great-grandfather and left him in the desert, linking her story to Stanley's family. Years later, Trout Walker and Linda Miller try to force Kate to reveal where her treasure is buried, but she dies from a lizard bite without telling them.

QuestionListen For
How is Kate connected to Stanley's family?She robbed Stanley's great-grandfather and left him in the desert.
What do Trout and Linda want from Kate?They want her to reveal where she buried her stolen treasure.
Why does Kate's ending deepen the treasure mystery?She dies without telling anyone where the treasure is, leaving the secret buried.

Chapter 29

Teacher — 1 sentence

A storm and a thumb-shaped mountain help Stanley connect old stories to the present.

Student reader — board word: STORM
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

7th-grade read-aloud

In the present, clouds gather and thunder rolls near Camp Green Lake. Stanley sees a mountain formation that looks like a thumb and remembers the family story about God's Thumb. The chapter connects the current timeline, family history, and the physical landscape.

QuestionListen For
What does Stanley notice in the distance?He notices a mountain formation that looks like a thumb.
How does this connect to his great-grandfather's story?It reminds him of God's Thumb, where his great-grandfather survived.
Why is this chapter important for multiple timelines?The present, old Green Lake, and Stanley's family story begin connecting in one landscape.
