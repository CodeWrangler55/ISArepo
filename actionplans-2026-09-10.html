7th/8th Grade Lit - Holes

Assigned reading for today: Holes, chapters 1-8.

If students read ahead: Use chapters 9-18 as deeper backup material.

Homework: Read chapters 9-18 of Holes.

Holes Chapters 1-8 Summaries Holes Chapters 9-18 Backup Summaries

Whiteboard Setup

Board Text How To Use It
Holes - Chapters 1-8 Focuses class on the assigned reading first, even if some students read ahead.
Big Question: What is unfair or suspicious at Camp Green Lake? Works for setting, character, conflict, and mystery.
Track: setting, mood, unfairness, power, mystery Use these words to keep the discussion analytical.
Closing: What do we know for sure, and what are we only guessing? Prevents spoilers and trains evidence-based thinking.

Class Flow

Time What To Do Purpose
0-5 min Attendance. Ask each student: "What is one thing that feels unfair, strange, or suspicious so far?" Starts with interpretation, not plot retelling.
5-12 min Quick class recap: Stanley arrives at Camp Green Lake, the camp is harsh, boys dig holes, Stanley may be innocent, the family curse is introduced, and Zero is underestimated. Gets everyone ready for discussion, including students who read unevenly.
12-40 min Work through the chapters 1-8 summary page. Read or paraphrase each chapter summary, then stop for 1-2 chapter questions. Fills the main class block with structured discussion tied to the assigned reading.
40-52 min If discussion is still strong, keep going with whole-assignment questions. If students are ahead or bored, open the chapters 9-18 backup page and preview the next layer of mystery. Gives flexible depth without losing control of spoilers.
52-58 min Closing discussion: "What do we know for sure, and what are we only guessing?" List answers in two columns. Separates evidence from prediction.
58-60 min Assign homework: read chapters 9-18. Tell students to watch for the gold tube, the Warden, and Zero. Gives the next reading a purpose.

Use These Questions First

Question Listen For
How does Camp Green Lake feel like a prison even without fences? The desert traps the boys because there is no water, no shade, and nowhere safe to run.
Is Stanley unlucky, treated unfairly, or both? Both. Strange things happen to him, but adults also judge him without really understanding him.
Why might the adults really want the boys to dig holes? The stated reason is character-building, but the rule about reporting interesting objects suggests they may be searching for something.
What kind of person is Stanley at the beginning? Lonely, insecure, unlucky, and used to being blamed, but also thoughtful and not cruel.

3rd Grade Lit - Charlotte's Web

Use this same plan for Literature Analysis (3-4) B and Literature Analysis (3-4) A.

Assigned reading for today: Charlotte's Web, chapters 11-17.

Lesson plan focus: Review chapters 11-17. Discuss Charlotte's plan and cause and effect.

Objective: Students will explain how Charlotte's words change what people think about Wilbur and identify cause-and-effect relationships.

Kid-Friendly Definitions

Word Definition Example
Cause Why something happens. Charlotte writes words in her web.
Effect What happens because of it. People think Wilbur is special.

Class Flow

Time What To Do Purpose
0-5 min Attendance. Ask: "What word did Charlotte write that you remember?" Starts with recall and gets them into the book quickly.
5-12 min Define cause and effect on the board. Use the simple example: Charlotte writes words, so people think Wilbur is special. Teaches the main thinking skill before discussion.
12-35 min Work through the chapter summaries below. After each summary, ask 1-2 questions and identify one cause/effect relationship. Keeps the class concrete and avoids relying only on memory.
35-50 min Use a handout or board activity for cause and effect. Keep the work simple and visual. Young students need a visible task, not just oral discussion.
50-57 min Share answers. Ask: "Which cause changed Wilbur's future the most?" Pushes students from matching facts into explaining importance.
57-60 min Assign next reading and preview that the fair will become very important. Closes with purpose for the next section.

Chapter Summary Refresher

Chapter What Happens Cause/Effect Cue
11 Charlotte writes "Some Pig" in her web. Lurvy, Mr. Zuckerman, and others are amazed. The people decide Wilbur must be special because the words appear above him. Cause: Charlotte writes the words. Effect: people admire Wilbur.
12 Charlotte calls a meeting of the barn animals. She needs help thinking of another word to write. The animals discuss how to keep helping Wilbur. Cause: Charlotte needs another idea. Effect: the animals meet and help plan.
13 Templeton brings scraps of paper with words. Charlotte studies the words and writes "Terrific" in her web. People continue believing Wilbur is amazing. Cause: Templeton brings words and Charlotte writes one. Effect: the plan keeps working.
14 Fern's mother worries because Fern talks about animals speaking. She visits Dr. Dorian, who stays calm and suggests Fern may be noticing things adults miss. Cause: Fern's mother worries. Effect: she asks Dr. Dorian for advice.
15 The crickets sing, showing that summer is ending. Wilbur is still alive, but time is passing and the fair is getting closer. Cause: summer is ending. Effect: the story feels more serious.
16 Wilbur is washed and prepared for the fair. Charlotte goes too, even though she is tired. Templeton comes because he expects food scraps. Cause: Wilbur goes to the fair. Effect: more people will see him.
17 At the fair, Wilbur meets another pig named Uncle. Uncle is very large, and Wilbur worries that Uncle might win instead of him. Cause: Wilbur sees Uncle. Effect: Wilbur becomes worried about the competition.

Question Bank

Question Listen For
What is Charlotte's plan? She writes words in her web so people will think Wilbur is special and worth saving.
Why do people think Wilbur is amazing? They see amazing words near him and assume the miracle is about Wilbur.
Who is really making the miracle happen? Charlotte is. The people notice Wilbur, but Charlotte is the one writing the words.
How does Templeton help Charlotte's plan? He brings scraps with printed words, which helps Charlotte choose what to write.
Why is the fair important? More people will see Wilbur, so Charlotte's plan can reach a bigger audience.
What new problem appears when Wilbur sees Uncle? Uncle is a large pig and might win, so Wilbur feels worried.
Give one cause and effect from these chapters. Example: Charlotte writes "Some Pig," so people come to admire Wilbur.

Possible Handouts For 3rd Grade

Handout Idea Why It Fits
Cause/Effect Matching: causes on the left, effects on the right, students draw lines. Best simple fit. It directly matches the objective and gives younger students a concrete task.
Charlotte's Word Power Chart: word Charlotte writes, what people think, how it helps Wilbur. Good if you want to focus more specifically on Charlotte's plan and the power of words.
Fair Poster: students make a simple poster advertising Wilbur using one strong describing word. More creative and fun, but less direct for cause and effect.
Cause/Effect Web: Charlotte's plan in the middle with effects around it. Good visual option, but it may lead many students to choose the same obvious answer.
Recommendation:

Use the cause/effect matching handout. It gives students enough structure, avoids blank-page confusion, and still lets you discuss why each match matters.
