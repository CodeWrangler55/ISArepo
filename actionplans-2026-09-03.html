3rd Grade Lit - Charlotte's Web

Use this same plan for Literature Analysis (3-4) B and Literature Analysis (3-4) A.

Assigned reading for today: Charlotte's Web, chapters 4-10.

Homework: Read chapters 11-17 of Charlotte's Web.

Whiteboard Setup

Board Text How To Use It
Charlotte's Web - Chapters 4-10 Sets the reading range and keeps the discussion from drifting into the ending.
Big Question: What makes someone a good friend? Use this to connect Wilbur's loneliness and Charlotte's promise.
Conflict: the big problem in a story Define before discussion. Student wording: "What is the trouble?"
Trait Words: lonely, kind, clever, patient, worried, loyal Students use these to describe Wilbur, Charlotte, Fern, or Templeton.
Answer Frame: "I think ___ is ___ because ___." Require the word "because" in spoken and written answers.

Class Flow

Time What To Do Purpose
0-5 min Attendance. Quick recap: Fern saved Wilbur, Wilbur moved to the barn, and Wilbur is learning what barn life is like. Supports students who are new to the book without slowing the rereaders down.
5-12 min Round-robin memory check: each student names one thing that happened in chapters 4-10. Write the events in order. Builds a shared plot map before analysis.
12-18 min Teach the word conflict. Ask: "What is Wilbur's trouble in these chapters?" Moves from summary into story structure.
18-35 min Discuss Wilbur's loneliness and Charlotte's friendship using the question bank below. Focuses on character feelings, friendship, and evidence.
35-48 min Partner or solo activity: Friend Evidence Chart. Students choose Wilbur or Charlotte and list two actions, one trait, and one because sentence. Creates a concrete product and fills the hour with accountable work.
48-55 min Share two or three charts. Correct vague answers by asking, "What in the chapter makes you think that?" Practices evidence-based speaking.
55-60 min Assign homework: read chapters 11-17. Preview: Charlotte will try to solve Wilbur's biggest problem. Gives students a reason to read.

Chapter Summary Refresher

Chapter What Happens Teaching Cue
4 Wilbur feels lonely in the barn. The other animals have their own routines, and he wants someone to play with. Loneliness makes the need for friendship concrete.
5 Wilbur hears a mysterious voice promising to be his friend. He eventually learns the voice belongs to Charlotte, a spider. Ask what makes Charlotte surprising as a friend.
6 The goslings hatch. One egg does not hatch, and Templeton takes it for himself. Good chapter for animal personalities and trait words.
7 Wilbur learns that Mr. Zuckerman plans to kill him for meat. Wilbur panics, and Charlotte promises to save him. This is the main conflict: Wilbur's life is in danger.
8 Fern's mother worries because Fern spends so much time with the animals and seems to understand them. Use carefully: Fern's imagination is part of the book's magic and point of view.
9 Charlotte explains how she spins a web. Wilbur tries to spin one too, but he cannot. Compare Wilbur and Charlotte: each has different strengths.
10 Avery almost catches Charlotte, but Templeton's rotten egg breaks and creates a terrible smell. Charlotte is safe. Cause and effect: the rotten egg unexpectedly protects Charlotte.

Question Bank

Question Listen For
Why is Wilbur lonely? He wants a friend, but the other animals are busy or uninterested.
What does Charlotte do that makes her seem like a good friend? She talks to Wilbur, listens, stays calm, and promises to help him.
What is the main conflict for Wilbur? He learns that he may be killed, so he needs help surviving.
How is Charlotte different from the other animals? She is thoughtful, patient, clever, and willing to make a plan.
What character trait would you give Templeton? Why? Selfish, sneaky, practical, or clever, as long as students support it with a reason.

Student Activity

Friend Evidence Chart Sentence Frame

Character: Wilbur or Charlotte

Action 1: __________

Action 2: __________

Trait: __________

I think __________ is __________ because __________.

A good friend is someone who __________.

Plan B If They Did Not Read

Give a fast teacher recap of chapters 4-10: Wilbur feels lonely in the barn, meets Charlotte, learns that his life is in danger, and Charlotte says she will help him.

Then run the Friend Evidence Chart from the recap. Do not spend the whole class retelling the chapters.

8th Grade Lit - Sarah, Plain and Tall / Holes

Assigned reading for today: Sarah, Plain and Tall, chapters 6-9.

Homework: Read chapters 1-8 of Holes.

Whiteboard Setup

Board Text How To Use It
Sarah, Plain and Tall - Chapters 6-9 Signals that today finishes the novel.
Big Question: Why does Sarah's choice matter? Keeps the discussion on character motivation and resolution.
Track: fear, belonging, home, change, trust Use these as theme words during discussion.
Transition: How can setting shape a character's life? Bridge from the prairie/sea contrast into Holes and Camp Green Lake.
Homework: Holes, chapters 1-8 Make sure every student writes the new book assignment down.

Class Flow

Time What To Do Purpose
0-5 min Attendance. Ask each student for one word that describes the ending mood. Starts with interpretation rather than plot summary.
5-15 min Rebuild the ending: What happens in chapters 6-9? Put five key events in order. Checks reading and creates a shared base.
15-32 min Discuss Sarah's decision, the family's fears, and the meaning of home. Finishes the novel through theme and character motivation.
32-43 min Quick written response: "Sarah chooses to stay because ___." Require two pieces of evidence. Gives written evidence practice before switching books.
43-53 min Introduce Holes: title, author, premise, and first setting contrast. Do not spoil the plot. Builds curiosity for the next reading assignment.
53-60 min Preview reading task: as they read chapters 1-8, track Stanley, Camp Green Lake, and anything that seems unfair. Provides a purpose for homework.

Chapter Summary Refresher

Chapter What Happens Teaching Cue
6 Sarah keeps learning prairie life. The children watch for signs that she might stay, but they also notice how much she misses the sea. Track Sarah's mixed feelings: belonging and homesickness.
7 Sarah becomes more involved with the family and farm. She shows independence and capability, not just gentleness. Ask how Sarah is changing the family's daily life.
8 Sarah goes to town alone. Anna and Caleb worry that she may not come back because they know she misses Maine. This is the emotional tension near the end: fear of being left.
9 Sarah returns. Her return shows that she has chosen the family and the prairie, even though she still loves and misses the sea. Resolution: home becomes connected to people, not only place.

Holes Preview Refresher

Holes begins with Stanley Yelnats, a boy sent to Camp Green Lake after being accused of stealing. The camp is not green and does not have a lake; it is a harsh desert place where boys dig holes every day.

For tomorrow's handoff, keep the preview focused on setting, unfairness, and mystery. Do not explain the larger family-history connections yet.

Question Bank

Question Listen For
Why are Anna and Caleb afraid Sarah will leave? They have started to love her and fear losing another mother figure.
What does Sarah miss about Maine? The sea, her brother, familiar places, and her old life.
What shows that Sarah is becoming part of the family? Her choices, her care for the children, and her decision to return/stay.
What does the book suggest home means? Home can be people and belonging, not only the place someone came from.
How might Holes be different from this book? Students may predict a harsher setting, more mystery, more conflict, or a different tone.

Quick Written Response

Prompt: Sarah chooses to stay because __________.

Evidence 1: __________

Evidence 2: __________

Final sentence: This shows that __________.

If Discussion Runs Short

3rd Grade Extension: Have students draw Wilbur and Charlotte. Under each drawing, write one trait and one action that proves it.

8th Grade Extension: Make a T-chart: "Sarah's old home" and "Sarah's new home." Students list details and infer what each place means to her.
