4th Grade First Day Literature Analysis

Book: Charlotte's Web by E. B. White. First class focus: Chapter 1, "Before Breakfast."

TAKE ATTENDANCE

Whiteboard Setup

Write this before students arrive.

Board Text How To Use It
Charlotte's Web - Chapter 1: Before Breakfast Sets the focus text for the day.
Literature Analysis = What happened? + What does it show? Use this when students only retell the plot.
Big Question: What do Fern's actions show about her? Keeps discussion centered on character analysis.
Characters: Fern, Wilbur, Mr. Arable, Mrs. Arable, Avery Helps students track the opening chapter.
Conflict: Fern wants ___, but Mr. Arable wants ___. Students fill in: Fern wants Wilbur saved; Mr. Arable thinks the runt should be killed.
Trait Words: brave, kind, fair, determined, responsible Gives students vocabulary for describing Fern.
Answer Frame: "I think ___ because ___." Prompts students to support answers with reasons.

First Day Flow

Time What To Do Purpose
0-5 min Welcome students, introduce yourself briefly, and explain that literature analysis means asking what a story shows us about characters, choices, problems, and meaning. Set a friendly, clear tone.
5-12 min Play Character Clue, described below. Learn names and connect the icebreaker to character clues in stories.
12-17 min Introduce vocabulary: character, setting, conflict, trait, evidence, protagonist. Give students words they can use immediately.
17-47 min Discuss Chapter 1 using the question bank. Practice moving from summary to simple analysis.
47-55 min Wrap up with: "What did we learn about Fern from what she did?" End with a concrete character-analysis takeaway.

Icebreaker Game: Character Clue

How it works: Each student says their name and gives one "character clue" about themselves: something they like, something they are curious about, or a small problem they would want solved in a story. Example: "I'm Maya, and my character clue is that I always notice tiny details."

Bridge into literature: "Good readers do the same thing with characters. We notice clues: what people say, what they do, what they want, and what problems they face."

Plan B: If Students Have Not Read Chapter 1

Use this if most or all students have not read yet. You can still teach character, conflict, trait, evidence, and theme using the opening situation.

What To Say

"Since this is our first meeting, some of you may not have had a chance to read Chapter 1 yet, and that is okay. Today we are going to practice the tools of literature analysis using the opening situation of Charlotte's Web."

"At the beginning of the story, a girl named Fern sees her father heading outside with an ax. She finds out that a tiny baby pig has been born. The pig is called a runt, which means it is smaller and weaker than the others. Fern's father thinks the practical thing to do is to kill the pig because it may not grow well. Fern is shocked. She thinks that is unfair. She speaks up, argues with her father, and insists that the pig's life matters even though he is small. Her father finally lets Fern keep the pig and care for him. Fern names him Wilbur."

"So even before we read every word, we already have a character, a problem, and a choice. That gives us something to analyze. The big question is not just, 'What happened?' The better question is, 'What does Fern's choice show about who she is?'"

Plan B Board Add-On

Board Text Use
Character + Problem + Choice = Analysis Use this as the main Plan B framework.
Fern's choice: She speaks up for Wilbur. Students use this as evidence for trait words.
This shows Fern is ___ because ___. Use as the answer frame.

Plan B Questions

Question Good Answer / Direction
What is the problem in the opening situation? Wilbur's life is in danger because he is small and weak.
Who has a choice to make? Fern has to decide whether to stay quiet or speak up. Mr. Arable also has to decide whether to kill the pig or listen to Fern.
What might Fern be feeling? Upset, worried, angry, protective, or determined.
What does it show about Fern if she speaks up? It shows she is brave, compassionate, fair-minded, and willing to defend someone weaker.
Is being small a good reason to think something has less value? Students may say no. This opens the theme of protecting the vulnerable and seeing value in the overlooked.

Plan B Mini Activity: Trait Evidence Match

  1. Write: Fern speaks up to her father to save Wilbur.
  2. Ask students to choose a trait word: brave, kind, fair, determined, responsible.
  3. Have them answer aloud using: "This shows Fern is ___ because ___."
  4. If time remains, ask students to invent a chapter title based only on the setup, then reveal the real title: "Before Breakfast."

Charlotte's Web Cheat Sheet

Whole-Book Plot Points

  • Fern Arable saves a runt pig from being killed and names him Wilbur.
  • Fern cares for Wilbur, but he is eventually sold to her uncle Homer Zuckerman's farm.
  • In the barn, Wilbur feels lonely and learns that he is being raised for slaughter.
  • Charlotte, a wise barn spider, befriends Wilbur and promises to help save him.
  • Charlotte writes words in her web that make people think Wilbur is special.
  • Wilbur becomes famous and is taken to the county fair.
  • Charlotte's work saves Wilbur's life, but Charlotte dies after laying her egg sac.
  • Wilbur protects Charlotte's egg sac, and some of her children remain with him, continuing the friendship's legacy.

Chapter 1 Summary

Chapter 1 begins with Fern seeing her father, Mr. Arable, heading out with an ax. Fern learns that a runt pig has been born and that her father plans to kill it because it is small and weak. Fern argues that this is unfair and pleads for the pig's life. Her father is moved by her concern and gives the pig to Fern to raise. Fern names the pig Wilbur and begins caring for him right away. The chapter introduces Fern as compassionate, brave, and willing to speak up for what she thinks is right.

Answers To The Prep Questions

Question Good Answer
Why does Fern want to save Wilbur? Fern believes it is unfair to kill Wilbur just because he is small. She sees his life as valuable and responds with compassion.
What does Fern's argument with her father show about her character? It shows she is brave, caring, determined, and willing to challenge an adult when she believes something is wrong.
What problem is introduced at the beginning of the story? Wilbur's life is in danger because he is the runt. The larger problem is whether a small, weak, or overlooked creature deserves protection and care.

30-Minute Discussion Bank

Question Strong Answer / Direction
Who seems like the protagonist in Chapter 1? Fern is the main focus of Chapter 1 because her choice drives the action. Wilbur will also become central as the story continues.
What is the conflict? Fern wants to save the runt pig, while her father thinks killing it is practical farm judgment.
How does Fern show courage? She runs outside, confronts her father, and speaks strongly even though he is the adult and she is upset.
Why does Mr. Arable change his mind? Fern's argument helps him see the pig through her eyes. He may also want Fern to learn responsibility by caring for Wilbur.
What does "runt" mean, and why does it matter? A runt is the smallest or weakest animal in a litter. It matters because Wilbur is judged by size before anyone sees his personality or worth.
What character trait best describes Fern? Compassionate is the strongest answer, but brave, determined, and protective also fit.
What is the setting, and why does it matter? The story begins on a farm, where animal life and practical decisions about animals are part of daily life. That makes Fern's reaction stand out.
What might this chapter foreshadow? It may foreshadow that Wilbur's life will be threatened again and that friendship or words may help save him.
What lesson or theme starts to appear? Possible themes: kindness, fairness, speaking up, protecting the vulnerable, and seeing value in the overlooked.
How is analysis different from summary here? Summary says Fern saves a pig. Analysis asks what Fern's actions reveal about her, why the conflict matters, and what idea the author may be introducing.

4th Grade Literature Vocabulary

Word Definition
ProtagonistThe main character or central character in a story.
CharacterA person, animal, or being in a story.
SettingWhere and when a story takes place.
PlotThe important events in a story.
ConflictThe main problem or struggle in a story.
ThemeA bigger lesson or idea in a story.
InferenceA smart guess based on clues in the text.
EvidenceA detail from the story that supports your answer.
TraitA word that describes what a character is like, such as brave or kind.
MotivationThe reason a character does something.
ForeshadowingA hint about something that may happen later.

Quick Teaching Reminders

  • Keep answers concrete: character actions, feelings, problems, fairness, and kindness.
  • Ask "What happened?" first only if students are lost. Then ask, "What does that show?"
  • When students give a vague answer, ask: "What part of the chapter makes you think that?"
  • Do not assign homework unless ISA or the parents tell you to. You can say, "Parents will guide reading expectations."
