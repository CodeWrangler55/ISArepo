Literature Analysis Summary Packet

The Tale of Despereaux, Chapters 9-18 — teacher summaries, student-reader summaries, and board words.

Volunteer reader routine: Assign one student to each chapter. The student writes the chapter's board word, reads the summary aloud, and then the class answers one question. The board words can be reused in the word search.

The Tale of Despereaux — Chapters 9-18

Chapter 9

Teacher — 1 sentence

Furlough finds Despereaux reading in the library and brings him toward the Mouse Council.

Student reader — board word: LIBRARY
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 9 finds Despereaux in the library. He is not nibbling paper or searching for crumbs. He is reading, and he is especially interested in the part of stories where things end happily ever after.

His brother Furlough comes to find him because Despereaux must go before the Mouse Council. Despereaux does not seem focused on the danger. He wants to talk about Princess Pea and about love. Furlough is frustrated because Despereaux still does not think like the other mice.

This chapter shows that Despereaux's bravery is mixed with innocence. He believes in stories, love, and happy endings. He does not yet understand how harshly the mouse community will judge him. For a third grader, the main idea is that Despereaux makes choices based on what he loves, even when others think those choices are dangerous.

For students, the important choice is that Despereaux keeps thinking like a story hero even when others think he is foolish. He is not brave because he is big or strong. He is brave because he believes love and happy endings matter, even before he knows how much trouble that belief will cause.

QuestionListen For
Where does Furlough find Despereaux?He finds him in the library.
What kind of ending does Despereaux care about?He cares about happily ever after.
Why is Furlough worried or frustrated?Despereaux is focused on love and stories instead of the danger he is in.

Chapter 10

Teacher — 1 sentence

Despereaux stands before the Mouse Council and refuses to say that loving the princess was wrong.

Student reader — board word: TRIAL
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 10 is Despereaux's trial. The mouse community gathers to hear what he has done. To the mice, his behavior is shocking: he went near humans, let Princess Pea touch him, spoke to her, and said he loved her.

The Head Mouse asks Despereaux to explain himself. Despereaux does not deny what happened. He tells the truth. He admits that he broke the rules, but he does not believe he did something wrong. He thinks love, honor, and beauty matter more than mouse law.

This is a brave choice because Despereaux could try to save himself by lying or pretending to be sorry. Instead, he stands by what he believes. The consequence is serious: the council sentences him to the dungeon. This chapter helps students see that bravery does not always mean winning right away. Sometimes bravery means telling the truth when the truth costs you something.

This is a good chapter for the word consequence. Despereaux's choice is telling the truth about his love for Pea. The consequence is being sent to the dungeon. Students can see that a brave choice can still have a painful result.

QuestionListen For
What is Despereaux accused of doing?He is accused of going near humans, speaking to Pea, and loving her.
Does Despereaux deny the charges?No. He tells the truth and says he acted out of love.
What consequence does he face?He is sentenced to the dungeon.

Chapter 11

Teacher — 1 sentence

The threadmaster marks Despereaux for the dungeon but quietly understands his love of stories.

Student reader — board word: THREAD
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 11 introduces the threadmaster. After the Mouse Council's sentence, Despereaux must wear a red thread around his neck. Among the mice, that thread shows that he has been sent to the dungeon, which is almost the same as a death sentence.

The threadmaster has to do his job, but he is not cruel like the council. He speaks to Despereaux with sadness. He even understands the kind of love Despereaux feels because he knows about stories, knights, bravery, courtesy, and honor.

This matters because Despereaux discovers that not every mouse thinks exactly the same way. The threadmaster cannot save him, but his words give Despereaux a little courage. The chapter shows that small kindnesses can matter when someone is afraid. Despereaux is still tiny and terrified, but he is beginning to hold onto the idea that brave, honorable love is real.

The threadmaster is a helpful character to discuss because he does not stop the punishment, but he does give Despereaux words that strengthen him. Sometimes encouragement does not remove danger, but it helps a person face danger with a little more courage.

QuestionListen For
What does the red thread mean?It marks Despereaux as sentenced to the dungeon.
How is the threadmaster different from the council?He is sad and kind, and he understands stories and honor.
How might the threadmaster's words help Despereaux?They give him courage and make him feel less alone.

Chapter 12

Teacher — 1 sentence

Despereaux says goodbye to his family as the mice prepare to send him into darkness.

Student reader — board word: ADIEU
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 12 is a goodbye chapter. The drumbeat changes, and Despereaux is brought closer to the moment when he will be taken to the dungeon. The sound feels frightening because everyone knows what the dungeon means for a mouse.

Despereaux sees his mother, Antoinette. She is dramatic and sorrowful, but she does not offer to take his place or protect him. Her goodbye word is adieu, which means farewell. The narrator helps readers notice that saying farewell is not the same as showing brave love.

Two hooded mice come to take Despereaux away. This chapter is sad because Despereaux is being rejected by his own community and even by his family. It also shows the cost of his choices. He followed music, stories, and the princess, and now he must face the consequence. He is scared, but the story keeps asking whether he can still be brave.

This chapter also helps students compare words and actions. Antoinette sounds sad, but she does not act to protect Despereaux. That lets students ask: what does real love do when someone is in trouble?

QuestionListen For
What word does Antoinette say to Despereaux?She says adieu, meaning farewell.
Why is this goodbye sad?His family does not rescue or defend him.
What consequence is Despereaux about to face?He is about to be taken to the dungeon.

Chapter 13

Teacher — 1 sentence

Furlough helps lead Despereaux to the dungeon, where Despereaux falls into darkness.

Student reader — board word: DUNGEON
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 13 follows Despereaux as he is led toward the dungeon. The red thread around his neck feels tight, and he knows he is going somewhere terrifying. He asks for a final word with Princess Pea, but the mice taking him do not show mercy.

One of the hooded mice is Furlough, Despereaux's own brother. That makes the betrayal feel worse. A brother should protect you, but Furlough helps carry out the punishment. He treats the sentence as if it is simply the rule.

When they reach the dungeon stairs, Despereaux is pushed or dropped into the darkness. Two words stay with him: perfidy and Pea. Perfidy means betrayal, and Pea is the princess he loves. This chapter makes the consequence real. Despereaux's brave choices have led him into danger, but his love and his sense of betrayal stay alive even in the dark.

The word perfidy is difficult, but the idea is simple: betrayal. Despereaux is betrayed by mice who should care about him. Still, he keeps thinking about Pea, which shows that love and hope have not disappeared.

QuestionListen For
Who helps lead Despereaux to the dungeon?Furlough and another hooded mouse lead him there.
Why does Furlough's role feel like betrayal?He is Despereaux's brother but helps punish him.
What two ideas stay with Despereaux?Perfidy, meaning betrayal, and Pea.

Chapter 14

Teacher — 1 sentence

Despereaux enters the dungeon and tries to comfort himself with story and bravery.

Student reader — board word: DARKNESS
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 14 brings Despereaux into the dungeon. It is dark, smelly, and frightening. The darkness is so deep that Despereaux almost feels as if he might disappear inside it. For a tiny mouse who loves light, music, and stories, the dungeon is the worst possible place.

Despereaux thinks about fainting, but he tries to remember the threadmaster's words: honor, courtesy, devotion, and bravery. Those words matter because they give him something to hold onto when he is afraid.

He begins to tell himself a story out loud. This is important because stories are one of Despereaux's sources of courage. Then a loud voice answers from the darkness, and Despereaux faints from fear. The chapter shows that being brave does not mean never being scared. Despereaux is very scared, but he still tries to face the darkness with the tools he has: words, stories, and hope.

This chapter is useful because Despereaux is obviously afraid. Third graders should know that fear does not cancel bravery. Despereaux's brave action is trying to remember good words and tell a story while he is surrounded by darkness.

QuestionListen For
What is the dungeon like?It is dark, smelly, frightening, and dangerous.
What words does Despereaux try to remember?Honor, courtesy, devotion, and bravery.
Does Despereaux stop being afraid?No. He is afraid, but he still tries to be brave.

Chapter 15

Teacher — 1 sentence

Gregory the jailer saves Despereaux because Despereaux can tell him a story.

Student reader — board word: STORY
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 15 introduces Gregory the jailer. Despereaux wakes up in Gregory's hand, still alive. Gregory lives and works in the dungeon, and he uses a rope tied to his ankle so he will not get lost in the darkness.

Gregory tells Despereaux that the dungeon rats are dangerous. He also shows Despereaux the pile of soup bowls and spoons that were thrown into the dungeon after Queen Rosemary died. This detail hints that the castle has a painful history connected to rats, soup, grief, and love.

Gregory agrees to help Despereaux, but he wants something in return: a story. He says stories are light, and light is precious in a dark world. This is one of the most important ideas in the book. Despereaux's love of stories is not silly. In the dungeon, a story can be a kind of light, and that light may help him survive.

Gregory's idea that stories are light connects directly to Despereaux's heart. The dungeon is dark, but a story can help someone imagine hope. Students can connect this to why Despereaux loves books so much.

QuestionListen For
Who is Gregory?He is the jailer who lives and works in the dungeon.
Why does Gregory use a rope?He uses it so he will not get lost in the dark dungeon.
Why are stories important to Gregory?He says stories are light in a dark world.

Chapter 16

Teacher — 1 sentence

The story shifts to Roscuro, a dungeon rat who becomes fascinated by light.

Student reader — board word: ROSCURO
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 16 begins a new part of the book and introduces a rat named Chiaroscuro, usually called Roscuro. He is born in the dungeon, a place of darkness. His name is important because chiaroscuro means the way light and dark appear together in art.

Roscuro is different from other rats. Rats are supposed to love darkness and enjoy causing suffering. But Roscuro becomes fascinated by light. When Gregory lights a match near him, the flame frightens him but also fills him with wonder.

This chapter gives students another character who is different. Despereaux is a mouse who loves light, music, stories, and a princess. Roscuro is a rat who is supposed to belong to darkness, but he longs for light. The chapter asks us to think about choices and identity. Does Roscuro have to act like other rats, or can he choose something different?

Roscuro is not Despereaux, but his story asks a similar question. What happens when someone wants something their community says they should not want? Roscuro's interest in light makes him unusual, just as Despereaux's love of stories made him unusual.

QuestionListen For
Who is introduced in this chapter?Roscuro, a rat born in the dungeon.
What makes Roscuro unusual for a rat?He becomes interested in light instead of only loving darkness.
How is Roscuro similar to Despereaux?Both are different from what their communities expect.

Chapter 17

Teacher — 1 sentence

Roscuro sees light from upstairs and longs to leave the darkness of the dungeon.

Student reader — board word: LIGHT
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 17 shows Roscuro watching a prisoner arrive in the dungeon. When the dungeon door opens, bright light pours down from the world upstairs. Botticelli, another rat, turns away from it, but Roscuro stares into it with wonder.

The light changes Roscuro. He sees beauty in it and wants more. He wants to go upstairs and see the bright world for himself. Botticelli reminds him that rats belong in darkness and that rats are supposed to make people suffer.

This chapter is about temptation and choice. Roscuro feels pulled between what he is told rats should be and what his heart wants. Like Despereaux, he is drawn toward something beautiful. But he is also surrounded by voices telling him to choose cruelty. Students can compare the two characters: both are unusual, but their choices may lead them in different directions.

Students can compare Botticelli and Roscuro. Botticelli tells Roscuro what rats are supposed to be. Roscuro feels something different inside himself. This sets up a choice between following others and listening to what he longs for.

QuestionListen For
What does Roscuro see when the dungeon door opens?He sees bright light from upstairs.
How does Botticelli react to the light?He dislikes it and says rats belong in darkness.
What choice is beginning for Roscuro?Whether to follow his longing for light or act like a cruel rat.

Chapter 18

Teacher — 1 sentence

Roscuro steals a prisoner's red cloth but realizes it cannot replace the light he wants.

Student reader — board word: CLOTH
Write this word on the board before or after reading the chapter summary.

Read-aloud summary — about 2 minutes

Chapter 18 shows Roscuro trying to act like the kind of rat Botticelli wants him to be. He goes to a prisoner in the dungeon and pretends to be a comforting voice. He asks the man to confess what he has done.

The prisoner admits that he stole cows, but he also tells a sadder truth: long ago, he traded his own daughter for a red cloth, a hen, and some cigarettes. That red cloth is almost all he has left. Roscuro takes the cloth away from him, trying to enjoy the cruelty of the trick.

But the cloth does not satisfy Roscuro. It is red and beautiful, but it is not the same as the light he saw from upstairs. This chapter shows a consequence of choices. Roscuro tries to choose darkness and cruelty, but his heart is still pulled toward light. The question is whether he will become better because of that longing or worse because he cannot have what he wants.

The red cloth is important because it is beautiful but not enough. Roscuro tries a cruel rat trick, but it does not give him what he really wants. This helps students see that choices can leave a character feeling worse, not better.

QuestionListen For
What does Roscuro take from the prisoner?He takes the prisoner's red cloth.
What sad thing does the prisoner confess?He traded his daughter for the cloth, a hen, and cigarettes.
Why does the cloth not satisfy Roscuro?It is not the same as the light he truly wants.
